# Niche, Acquisition & Distribution

**Version:** 26.07.29  
**Audience:** Internal ops, founders, content, partnerships, and AI agents drafting Synth strategy or GTM copy.

This document is the deep niche brief for Synth. Use it when planning growth, partner outreach, content calendars, or explaining what category Synth competes in.

---

## 1. Category definition

Synth sits in **live music social discovery** — not ticketing, not streaming, not generic event listings.

| Adjacent category | What they own | What they leave open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticketing (Ticketmaster, AXS, Dice) | Inventory, checkout, seats | Taste identity, post-show memory, who to go with |
| Streaming (Spotify, Apple Music) | Songs, playlists, listening graphs | Physical nights, venues, companion-finding |
| Listing apps (Bandsintown, Songkick) | “Who’s coming to town” alerts | Social proof from friends, reviews, passport |
| Social feeds (Instagram, TikTok) | Attention and clips | Structured show history, trust, local depth |
| Chat (Discord, GroupMe) | Ephemeral conversation | Discovery, catalog, public taste identity |

**Public framing:** the Letterboxd for live music.  
**Product line:** Discover, Connect, Share.

Synth’s job is to own the **full live night loop**: find the show → find people → go → review while memory is fresh → keep a passport worth returning to → discover the next night from friends and taste.

## 2. Niche depth: who we serve and why it hurts

### 2.1 Core insight

Live music demand is high, but the **social and memory layer is broken**. Fans already buy tickets and stream artists. They do not have a durable place that connects:

- upcoming shows in their city
- people with overlapping taste who actually go out
- a public record of nights that mattered
- editorial context about venues and scenes (especially outside the biggest acts)

That gap is Synth’s niche: **identity + companionship + archive around live music**, starting metro-by-metro.

### 2.2 Primary users (consumer)

1. **Taste-forward concert-goers (roughly 18–34)** who treat live music as identity, not occasional entertainment. They already follow local venues, save IG Stories of setlists, and debate openers in group chats.
2. **Solo-or-almost-solo goers** who miss shows because their friends are busy, or who go alone and wish the night felt less anonymous.
3. **Scene locals** who know rooms, openers, and neighborhood quirks — and want their knowledge recognized (passport, reviews, chats).
4. **Transplants and travelers** who need a trustworthy local map of rooms and people, not just a Ticketmaster search.

### 2.3 Secondary users (B2B / distribution partners)

1. **Independent venues and promoters** who need filled rooms, younger discovery channels, and fans who come back — without becoming another ads vendor.
2. **Artists, managers, and local acts** who want verified presence and direct fan context beyond a feed algorithm.
3. **Campus orgs, college radio, and student ambassadors** who already aggregate shows for peers.
4. **Local music media / Substack writers** who need distribution and a living archive of the scene.
5. **Lifestyle brands and tourism** that want credible association with live culture, not generic “music sponsorship.”

### 2.4 Geographic wedge

**DC / DMV first.** Dense venue ecology, strong college population, political + cultural transient audience, and enough mid-size rooms to make editorial content concrete (9:30 Club, Black Cat, Union Stage, Anthem, jazz rooms, DIY, etc.).

Expansion pattern: prove density in one metro (events catalog + reviews + social graph + editorial) → clone the playbook city by city. Content and partnerships should sound **local and specific**, never generically “music lovers everywhere.”

### 2.5 Why now

- Streaming made taste portable; live nights are the scarce, high-emotion complement.
- Loneliness and “who do I go with?” are mainstream, not niche.
- Passport / status / archive products (Letterboxd, Strava, Goodreads) trained users to log culture publicly.
- Short-form video made venue and show clips viral — but memory still evaporates into camera rolls.
- Venues need owned relationships with fans as paid acquisition gets more expensive.

### 2.6 Positioning tests (use in briefs)

A piece of content or partner pitch is on-niche if it strengthens at least one of:

1. **Discover** — specific show, room, or scene knowledge
2. **Connect** — finding people or community around taste
3. **Share** — review, passport stamp, memory worth keeping

Off-niche examples to avoid: pure ticket price dumps, streaming-playlist tips with no live angle, generic nightlife promo with no music identity.

## 3. User acquisition channels

Acquisition is dual-track: **consumer demand** and **distribution partners** that bring audiences Synth cannot buy cheaply.

### 3.1 Consumer channels (ranked by strategic fit)

| Channel | Role | What works | What fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram** | Consumer awareness + venue visual proof | Show/venue specifics, passport moments, friend tags, Stories from nights | Vague “community” captions, hashtag stuffing |
| **TikTok / short Reels** | Top-of-funnel curiosity | Hooks about solo shows, venue tips, “what I’d tell a transplant,” scene myths | Corporate explainers, long product tours |
| **Substack (long form)** | Trust + SEO + scene authority | Sourced venue essays, metro playbooks, founder thesis | Recycled social captions padded to 800 words |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B + talent + partners | Operator lessons, market structure, founder narrative | Consumer memes, ticket spam |
| **Reddit (local / music)** | High-trust local discovery | Useful answers in r/washingtondc and genre subs; no hard sell | Link-only posts, fake enthusiasm |
| **App Store / ASO** | Capture intent | “Letterboxd for live music,” concert passport, find people for shows | Competing on “tickets” keywords we do not own |
| **Campus / ambassadors** | Dense early graph | GWU and DC schools; club nights; poster + QR to join | One-off tabling with no follow-up content |
| **Friend invites & chats** | Network effects | Genre chats, group plans, tagging friends in reviews | Empty social features without local density |
| **Streaming sync (Spotify / Apple)** | Activation & retention | Taste onboarding that maps to upcoming local shows | Treating sync as the product instead of a signal |
| **Local press & blogs** | Credibility | Founder story, metro launch, venue partnerships | National vanity press before local proof |

### 3.2 Funnel logic (consumer)

1. **Attention** — short-form clip or IG carousel about a real DC show/venue/problem (“missed another show because friends flaked”).
2. **Belief** — Substack or LinkedIn piece that proves Synth understands the scene and the product job.
3. **Install / join** — App Store or join.getsynth.app with a clear first action (pick city/scene, artists, upcoming show).
4. **First value** — see a relevant upcoming show + a person or chat that makes going feel possible.
5. **Habit** — review after the night + passport stamp; invite a friend.

Content should usually optimize for steps 1–3; product and community ops own 4–5.

### 3.3 Distribution partner channels

Partners are not “sponsors who post once.” They are **pipes that repeatedly put Synth in front of the right people**.

| Partner type | Offer from Synth | Ask from partner | Content / GTM motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Independent venues** | Editorial features, event accuracy, fan discovery, optional QR at doors | Mention in email/IG, poster placement, guest-list or soft ticket perk for early users | Venue deep-dives (Substack), IG room photography, LinkedIn on room economics |
| **Promoters / bookers** | Audience context, show amplification for underplayed bills | Inclusion in advance posts, calendar accuracy | “Why this bill matters” shorts |
| **Artists / managers (esp. local)** | Verified presence, fan reviews, show pages | Bio link, Story shout, post-show CTA | Artist-in-the-room stories; passport stamps |
| **College orgs / radio** | Campus ambassador kit, exclusive chats | Weekly show roundups, table events | Campus-night Reels; ambassador LinkedIn |
| **Local music writers** | Cross-publish, research access, audience | Newsletter swaps, co-bylines | Substack collaborations |
| **Lifestyle / audio / beverage brands** | Credible live-culture association | Experiential budgets, metro activations | B2B LinkedIn + event content |
| **Tourism / city culture orgs** | Visitor discovery layer for live music | Guides, widgets, campaign inclusion | “DC live music for transplants” packs |
| **Complementary apps** (not ticketing competitors) | Deep links into show pages | Reciprocal discovery | Product partnership posts |

### 3.4 Partner acquisition playbook (practical)

1. Lead with **usefulness to their room or audience**, not with “we’re building community.”
2. Show a **specific artifact**: a drafted venue essay, a sample IG carousel about their next three shows, a campus flyer mock.
3. Ask for a **repeatable placement** (email footer, door QR, monthly Story) instead of a one-time announcement.
4. Measure partner value as **qualified joins from their metro + return visits to their events**, not vanity impressions.
5. Keep consumer voice and partner voice separate: LinkedIn/email for operators; IG/TikTok/Substack for fans.

## 4. Channel jobs (content system)

Use these jobs when routing ideas through the Content Calendar idea reservoir.

| Bucket | Platforms | Audience | Editorial job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B2B / operator** | LinkedIn (primary), email, partner decks | Venues, promoters, campus, brands, talent | Teach a lesson about live music markets, rooms, or product; invite partnership conversation |
| **Consumer short-form** | Instagram, TikTok / Reels | Fans, students, transplants | Create recognition, feeling, or a saveable tip in under ~90 seconds / 140 words |
| **Consumer long-form** | Substack | Scene-curious readers, SEO, partners who skim | Argue a thesis with sources; leave the reader smarter about a room, night, or metro |

Reddit remains a **trust channel**, not a dump pipe: useful local answers only.

## 5. Messages that convert (by audience)

### Fans

- Going alone is normal — finding your people should be easier.
- Your shows deserve a passport, not a buried camera roll.
- Local rooms have stories; Synth helps you find the next one that fits your taste.

### Venues / promoters

- We send fans who already care about live music identity, not random nightlife traffic.
- Editorial + accurate listings make your room legible to newcomers.
- Soft asks beat hard ads: QR, email footer, co-authored scene posts.

### Campus

- Be the person who always knows what’s on this week — and who else is going.
- Ambassadors seed the social graph before paid spend makes sense.

### Brands

- Live nights are high-emotion and local; association beats banner ads if the cultural fit is real.

---

## 6. Anti-goals (keep the niche sharp)

- Do not position Synth as a ticket marketplace.
- Do not chase national celebrity gossip over local scene depth.
- Do not publish empty “vibrant community” copy without a concrete show, room, or person.
- Do not treat every platform as the same caption at different lengths.
- Do not invent metrics (MAU, revenue, fundraising) in public or partner materials.

## 7. How this feeds the Content Calendar

1. **Level 1** — umbrella thought-leadership angles from this niche brief.
2. **Level 2** — sort each angle into B2B LinkedIn, consumer short-form (IG/TikTok), or long-form Substack.
3. **Level 3** — specific ideas with angle, channel, person (voice), and draft copy.

When in doubt, prefer **DC-specific, evidence-backed, platform-native** drafts over generic inspiration quotes.
